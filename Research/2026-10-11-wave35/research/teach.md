# Teaching, pickups and pacing for a 6-level campaign (wave 35 research)

Web research, 2026-10-11. "Fetched" means the page itself was read; "snippet" means only a search engine summary of it
was read. Nothing here was checked in a running game.

## Answer

The best action games teach one verb per room or level, show it first (ULTRAKILL's room-by-room text with the bound
key, Titanfall 2's hologram pilot), then make the player do it at once in a space where failing is cheap, then test it
(Nintendo's introduce / develop / twist / conclude; Portal's "gating"; the boss as the level's exam). Text works when
it is one short imperative sentence naming the input as the player has it bound, in the device's own glyph, and the
room cannot be left until the verb is used. Pickups read by silhouette and value first, colour second: the real model
(or its outline, as CoD Zombies' chalk wall buys), a bob or spin, glow, and a colour family per kind (Neon White's
cards, Terraria's rarity colours, Doom Eternal's glowing supplies). A red cross is off limits (the ICRC asks games not
to use it). For 6 levels: core verbs in level 1, one new verb per later level, the grapple at 4, the jump-dash shown at
5, level 6 a mixed final exam, and each level's boss tests only what that level taught.

## 1. In-level tutorial text

**ULTRAKILL (fetched, https://ultrakill.wiki.gg/wiki/0-1).** 0-1 is split into a Tutorial and the main level. Each
tutorial room isolates one mechanic, gated by an obstacle that needs it: punch (a wooden blockade), slide (a jammed
door), dash (gaps and electric hazards), blood healing, wall jump (raised platforms), ground slam (breakable floor),
projectile deflection, the revolver's charge shot. The text names the bound key and the rule in capitals:
"Press 'F' to PUNCH." / "Hold 'Left Control' to SLIDE." / "Press 'Left Shift' to DASH through danger. Consumes
STAMINA. Can be performed in air." / "JUMP while near a WALL to WALL JUMP. (Max. 3 times)" / "Deal close range damage
to douse yourself in FRESH BLOOD. THIS IS THE ONLY WAY TO REGAIN HEALTH." Pattern: verb in caps, the input, then at
most one rule (cost, limit, or "only way"). Whether the text sits on a HUD popup or on a wall, what triggers it, and
when it disappears: not found on that page.

**Titanfall 2's Gauntlet.** A hologram pilot shows how to clear each obstacle, for example sprinting and jumping at a
wall at an angle to wallrun (snippet, https://i1.trueachievements.com/game/Titanfall-2/walkthrough/2). It ends as a
timed course against your personal best, so the tutorial is replayable practice, not a lecture (snippet,
https://www.tuftsdaily.com/article/2018/02/art-games-tutorials-overlooked-aspect-games). Respawn built levels with
"action blocks" (rapid prototypes of one idea) and one standout idea per level, for example Effect and Cause (fetched
abstract, https://gdcvault.com/play/1025105/Designing-Unforgettable-Titanfall-Single-Player).

**Portal (fetched, https://theportalwiki.com/wiki/Portal_developer_commentary).** "Portal is effectively an extended
player training exercise ... introducing a series of gameplay tools, then layering these tools into increasingly
difficult puzzles." Chamber 01 uses "gating, in which a solid understanding of key gameplay concepts is required for
success." Momentum was "introduce[d] ... slowly, step by step." Two new skills at once "proved to be too much ...
Overwhelmed players tend not to digest new information." Clutter "interfered with the portal training process", so
teaching rooms were stripped bare.

**Celeste (snippet, https://game-wisdom.com/analysis/celeste).** Prologue adds climb and dash; each chapter adds one
hazard or rule; compact rooms keep the goal in sight. **Neon White (snippets,
https://indiegamefans.com/neon-white-pc-review-faster-than-a-speeding-bullet/,
https://www.thegamer.com/neon-white-soul-cards-tips-tricks-guide/):** each card introduced in isolation, then ramped;
hints unlock after attempts. **Terraria (snippet, https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_progression):** no quest log;
gear tiers gated by bosses; each new area brings new enemies and loot.

**CoD Zombies' first-round prompts:** not found (no source read on how round 1 prompts are worded or timed).

**Best practices (fetched, https://www.wayline.io/blog/demystifying-game-tutorials):** let players experiment; teach
when the mechanic becomes relevant; show, don't tell; break systems into chunks; "Make failure safe. Allow players to
retry until they succeed."; give clear success / fail feedback. Snippet
(https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-design-a-tutorial-level): one mechanic at a time, act within seconds, text boxes are
"a patch for levels that failed to teach".

**Pad glyphs (fetched, https://devdocs.xbox.com/build/game-principles/accessibility/xag-deep-dives/xag-107-input.md):**
"Remapped bindings update hints, tips, tutorials, and controller diagrams." Avoid long holds; offer toggles.
Prompts swapping to the detected device mid-session: snippet only (same search, a third-party patterns file).

**Common mistakes (fetched, Wayline above):** "Walls of text will be skipped"; front-loading; forced linear chores;
poor feedback; no practice after the lesson. Portal adds: two new verbs at once, and clutter in a teaching room.

## 2. Pickup readability

**Silhouette and value first (snippet,
https://bugnet.io/blog/readable-game-art-why-clarity-beats-detail; the fetched page was an index, so not confirmed).**
Players recognise outline before colour; every pickup should read as a filled shape; threats, player and
interactables should sit in a different brightness band from the level; a bob makes pickups visible on noisy floors;
glow helps discovery; beneficial pickups can be green or cyan.

**Doom Eternal (snippets).** Supplies sit in arenas or drop from enemies on glory kill, blood punch, flame belch,
chainsaw (https://doomwiki.org/wiki/Supplies_(Doom_Eternal), fetch refused 403). Weakened enemies flash orange to
invite a glory kill (https://www.inverse.com/gaming/10-doom-eternal-details-we-learned-after-playing-the-first-three-hours/amp).
Its colourful pickups and green helmets drew "cartoonish" complaints, answered with an amber HUD preset and a pickup
toggle (https://gameinformer.com/preview/2019/06/13/id-talks-mod-support-integrating-more-traversal-and-the-controversial-neon-ui).

**Neon White (snippets, sources above).** Weapons are cards so colourful you know your gun by colour alone, even in
peripheral vision; each card also has a discard verb (extra jump, dash, grapple).

**CoD Zombies wall buys.** In Buried, weapons are chalk drawings on the wall; the player draws a chosen weapon's
outline on a "?" space, which then sells that gun for the rest of the game (snippet,
https://callofduty.fandom.com/wiki/Chalk; also https://www.accelerated-ideas.com/news/what-are-the-chalk-question-marks-on-wall-black-ops-2-zombies-buried.aspx).
That the classic wall buy is a chalk outline that shows the gun model after purchase: not confirmed (the Wall Weapons
wiki page refused the fetch).

**Terraria rarity (snippet, https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Rarity).** Item value is shown by name text colour, from
gray (-1) and white (0) through blue, green, orange, light red, pink, light purple, lime, yellow, cyan, red, purple
(11), with animated colours for Expert / Master items.

**Health symbol (snippets, https://www.pcworld.com/article/1989513/did-you-know-that-this-symbol-is-forbidden-in-video-games.html,
https://thenextweb.com/news/video-games-red-cross-trouble).** The red cross on white is a protected emblem under the
Geneva Conventions; the Red Cross has asked developers not to use it (Prison Architect was contacted); Halo moved to
an "H". Health reading as health needs another shape (a heart, a plus in a non-red colour, a vial). Rotating
pickups and floating pickup names: no design source found beyond the bob / glow snippet above.

## 3. Campaign pacing

**Kishotenketsu (fetched, https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-secret-to-i-mario-i-level-design).** Koichi
Hayashida: learn the mechanic, then "a slightly more complicated scenario in which you have to use it", then a twist,
then mastery. Miyamoto's question is the third step: "That twist that really surprises people." A snippet
(https://www.mcvuk.com/development/video-nintendos-level-design-secrets-in-four-steps) adds that a stage can teach,
develop, twist and discard a mechanic "in about five minutes flat".

**Boss as the final exam (fetched, https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/boss-battles-culminations-of-an-idea).**
"Boss battles should be the culmination of what you learned throughout the stage." Mismatch "is like you spend an
hour teaching someone math and then quiz them on history." Mega Man: an ice-sliding stage gets an ice-sliding boss.

**Movement unlocks (snippet, https://allthings.how/metroidvania-explained-design-pillars-history-scope/ and a dev
log in the same search).** Double jump, dash and grapple each change how you read the architecture; games start slow
and each unlock makes traversal faster; tease spots that need a later verb before it unlocks; send the player back
through an old space with the new verb so the gain is felt.

**Unlock slots in 6-level games, boss-rush structure:** not found. The plan below is inferred, not measured.

## Rules for our 6-level campaign

1. One new verb per level, never two at once; level 1 may hold the core set only as a room-by-room tutorial
   (ULTRAKILL 0-1, https://ultrakill.wiki.gg/wiki/0-1; Portal ch. 15, https://theportalwiki.com/wiki/Portal_developer_commentary).
2. Each teaching room: text, then a blocker the verb alone clears, then a cheap retry (ULTRAKILL above; Wayline,
   https://www.wayline.io/blog/demystifying-game-tutorials).
3. Text format: VERB in caps, the bound input as a glyph, at most one rule; no paragraph (ULTRAKILL above; Wayline).
4. The glyph comes from the live binding and the active device, and updates on rebind (XAG 107,
   https://devdocs.xbox.com/build/game-principles/accessibility/xag-deep-dives/xag-107-input.md).
5. Show before telling where text fails: a ghost or a demo run of the verb (Titanfall 2,
   https://i1.trueachievements.com/game/Titanfall-2/walkthrough/2).
6. Each level runs introduce, develop, twist, conclude around its one verb (Hayashida,
   https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-secret-to-i-mario-i-level-design).
7. Each level's boss tests that level's verb and the ones before it, nothing untaught (Game Developer,
   https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/boss-battles-culminations-of-an-idea).
8. Grapple at level 4; tease grapple points in levels 2-3 that cannot be reached yet; level 4 sends the player back
   through a familiar shape with it (metroidvania snippet, https://allthings.how/metroidvania-explained-design-pillars-history-scope/).
9. Jump-dash shown at level 5 as the twist on dash plus grapple; level 6 is the exam mixing every verb (Hayashida; Game
   Developer boss article).
10. Teaching rooms are bare: no props or enemies that pull the eye from the verb (Portal ch. 05, source in rule 1).
11. Every pickup kind has its own silhouette at a glance; colour is a second cue, not the only one (bugnet snippet,
    https://bugnet.io/blog/readable-game-art-why-clarity-beats-detail).
12. Weapon pickups show the real gun model (or its outline on a wall buy), bobbing and glowing; one colour family per
    weapon so the player can tell it in peripheral vision (CoD chalk, https://callofduty.fandom.com/wiki/Chalk; Neon
    White, https://indiegamefans.com/neon-white-pc-review-faster-than-a-speeding-bullet/).
13. Health is never a red cross; use a non-cross shape, and keep pickup colours inside the dusk rules (cyan
    player-usable, never red-orange) (PCWorld, https://www.pcworld.com/article/1989513/did-you-know-that-this-symbol-is-forbidden-in-video-games.html;
    CLAUDE.md rule 8).
14. Make glow strength a setting; loud pickups read but draw complaints (Doom Eternal,
    https://gameinformer.com/preview/2019/06/13/id-talks-mod-support-integrating-more-traversal-and-the-controversial-neon-ui).
15. Tiers ride the name colour, Terraria style (https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Rarity); the tutorial replays as a timed
    run (Titanfall 2, https://www.tuftsdaily.com/article/2018/02/art-games-tutorials-overlooked-aspect-games).
