# DESIGN24-bosses.md: the three boss designs the master prompt builds from (the forge's synthesis of R1 plus the repo)

Sources: `R1-bosses.md` (every claim there carries a URL), the repo's own boss code (`Core/Enemies/Bulwark.cs` 847 lines, `BossKit.cs`, `BossMath.cs`), and the web boss files (frozen, read only: `skygrind/src/enemies/boss.js` MONOLITH, `hive.js` HIVE, `orbiter.js` ORBITER).
House rules that shape every boss: fixed 120 Hz step, timers in whole steps (`stepsFor`), Mulberry32 per run, no PhysX, hit tests on the C# collider, every number from config (`design.boss.<id>.*` with `_why`), enemy models are boxes, named events (`bossIntro`, `bossBeat`, `bossTell`, `bossPhase`, `bossHit`, `bossPart`, `bossDying`, `bossDown`: the web's names, `PORT-STATUS.md`), no audio code. **Rule for every boss (B6): it teaches one movement skill.**

## 1. THE KILN WYRM (worm boss; built tonight)  [GG: "a crazy worm boss ... slithers really easy ... really fast", twice]
- **Look:** 24 body boxes + head + tail = 26 pieces. Head 3x3x4 m, body 2.5 m tapering to 1.2 m. Pale-violet boundary trim, red-orange glowing core on the head only while exposed (enemies and danger are red-orange: rule 8).
- **Body:** ring buffer of head positions, one per step; piece i reads the buffer `i * spacingSteps` steps back. Exact, deterministic, no physics. Each piece is an oriented box from the direction to its neighbour.
- **HP:** one shared pool (the Destroyer rule: no shot is wasted). Body hit x1, head x2, exposed core x3. Body pieces are solid to SHOTS only (a ray against each piece's oriented box, in Wyrm code) and never die; the player is not blocked by them (the collider's BoxLayer is axis-aligned: no collider changes tonight): a piece's box test gives contact damage and a knockback. (Skip the tail-shed sub-boss if late.)
- **Speed:** base 22 m/s, +25 % in phase 2 and again in phase 3 (the Wall of Flesh ramp); the player's sprint is about 9 m/s and boosted movement 20-35 m/s, so the worm is "really fast" but a mastered player can ride it. All from config.
- **Path (head, a fixed learnable cycle of four patterns; every pattern is a waypoint list in `Design/bosses/wyrm.json`, Catmull-Rom smoothed, pitch clamped):** (1) SPIRAL COIL around the arena, rising 1 m per lap (a ramp the player can ride); (2) DIVE: burrows into the floor and erupts from a marked wall spot 4 s later (a floor crack and dust 1 s ahead); (3) LUNGE: a straight fast pass across at head height (jump the body or slide under); (4) LOOP through the central ring.
- **Attacks (every one has a tell of 0.5 s or more, colour and shape, no two resolve in the same 0.5 s):** lunge (head contact: high damage), spit barrage from body ports (a spiral, every 10th aimed), terrain-cross shards (5 shards burst when the head enters or leaves a wall), phase 3 squeeze (the coil radius closes toward a gap).
- **Phases (config thresholds):** P1 100-66 % slow, learn the cycle; P2 66-33 % spit starts, +25 % speed, the core exposes after a roar tell; P3 33-0 % the floor lowers to platforms or the coil squeezes, +25 % speed. The kill: the BossKit's slow-mo finisher, `bossDown`.
- **Arena:** a cylinder 60 m across and 30 m high (a closed room: the boss room of layer 1), 4-6 pillars, 3 platforms, wall surfaces to kick from. The worm stays at least 6 m clear of the player spawn.
- **Never cheap:** body contact hurts little, head contact hurts; burrow exits marked 1 s or more ahead; no off-screen spawns; a checkpoint at the room door and at each phase start.
- **Fight time target:** 2.5 to 3.5 min (a design target; R1 found no sourced norm). **Teaches:** slide and jump over passes, ride the coil, dash through gaps.
- **What the built worm taught (NIGHT24 builder B; all from `wyrm`, `wyrm --human`, `wyrm --shed` in SimHarness, seed 7 unless said; the numbers are in `design.n24.wyrm`, the paths in `Design/bosses/wyrm.json`):**
  - **HP pool:** 12500 first (the omniscient bot, 96 % of shots hitting, killed it in 3:36; the lag-limited `--human` bot took 7:20 to 8:08). 5600 puts the human bot at 3:08 / 3:10 / 3:03 (3 seeds, 0 deaths) and the omniscient bot at 1:45. So the pool is tuned to the human bot, and a player who reads the cycle and aims well can finish in under two minutes. Neither bot is a person; GG's hands are the measurement.
  - **Contact:** both bots took 0 body, head or lunge contacts in whole fights (they dodge by predicted or extrapolated position); the damage they took was the spit (7 to 25 hits of 8). The contact path is covered by unit tests only. The worm may be too easy to avoid by position; whether it is for a human on a pad is not measured.
  - **Core:** the human bot landed 0 to 1 core hits in 3 fights (core open 3.5 to 4.5 s, a moving target, 0.25 s aim lag). The x3 core is a reward for a good player, not a thing the pool counts on.
  - **Dive:** floor crack to eruption measured 3.7 to 5.4 s (design: about 4 s); the wall spot is marked 1.0 s ahead. 47 attacks in 3:36, every tell at least 0.5 s ahead, the nearest two resolves exactly 0.5 s apart (the gate holds, it is not slack).
  - **Rules found by running it:** a mark that exists only in phase 3 (the squeeze) must exist only on a path built in phase 3, else it resolves with no tell; the phase-3 coil closes through the gap between two pillars (a plain radius shrink crossed them); the body never overlaps the spawn nook (nearest piece 8.8 m over 30000 sampled steps in three fights).
  - **Tail-shed (built, off by default `tailShed.enabled`):** at each beat the last 5 pieces cut loose for 500 HP and regrow after 12 s; shot down they are gone and the worm takes 4 % of max HP. The human bot shot it down 3 of 4 times; the omniscient bot 0 of 4 (it moves away from it). Fight times with it on: bot 1:44 to 1:53, human 3:04 to 3:19.
- **Proof (contract):** a headless fight run: the bot kills it in a measured time at a fixed seed; the reel `wyrm` shows intro card, coil, dive telegraph and the finisher; fingerprint EQUAL with the row off; a step-exact tape replays EQUAL.

## 2. THE OUTRUNNER (highway chase boss; designed tonight, built NIGHT25)  [GG: "an endless highway ... you're chasing the boss ... Sonic ... can't just move right to left"]
- **Open question for GG (D-10):** the only JoJo Part 5 highway fight R1 could verify is Giorno and Mista versus Ghiaccio on the Venice bridge, where the boss pursues the car. GG says the player chases. The design supports both directions with one rule: a single **gap meter** (player minus boss distance along the road).
- **Track:** a straight road streamed in 40 m tiles recycled behind the player (the world does not scroll: no float drift, deterministic). The boss runs ahead at base speed; the player's boosted movement is 1.4x or more.
- **Gap meter:** starts at 60 m. The boss can be hurt only at gap 25 m or less. At 80 m the boss "escapes": a soft fail, back to a checkpoint (never an instant death from one hit). The boss rubber-bands in ONE direction only (faster when you are far, never slower when near) and the meter says so (a silent rubber band is the Mario Kart complaint, R1 section 2.2).
- **Needs the full 3D kit (his "you can't just move right to left"):** two decks (overpass and tunnel) joined by ramps; broken-road gaps of 6 to 14 m (jump, dash, slide-jump); a wall to kick along that skips a hazard strip; grapple nodes on gantries; oil patches (less control) and boost strips (speed) so the player picks a line; boss attacks that need a move (low sweep: jump; high sweep: slide; ground slam: be airborne; a net: dash); damage counts only while airborne, sliding or above a speed threshold ("speed pays").
- **Read at speed:** world-side cues, not screen-border lines (retired in w20): road markings, light streaks, a FOV kick, a boss trail; hazards drawn 1.5 to 2 s ahead; lit guide strips for the intended line.
- **Boss:** six shield plates broken by sustained shots or a melee, then a finisher dash into the weak point. Phases: straight road with static hazards; gaps and the overpass; the boss drops mines and barricades and the road splits.
- **Prototype stub tonight only if everything else is DONE:** 400 m straight road, tile recycling, the gap meter and a boss box that runs; no hazards.
