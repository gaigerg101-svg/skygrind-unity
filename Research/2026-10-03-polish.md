# research-polish.md: the polish that makes top shooters stand out, and what SKYGRIND builds next

Research for GG, 2026-10-03. The question: what polish makes the best-selling, best-reviewed action / shooter games
stand out, and which of it would most raise SKYGRIND's quality bar. Scope: what to build and how it should feel (not
which Unity API; not retention systems).

**Tags:** [W] a page I fetched (URL in section 5) · [S] a search-result snippet only · [U] my inference or proposal ·
[V] read in the SKYGRIND repo (code, `PortExport/config.json`, `Logs/shots/`), as DESIGN-RESEARCH.md uses it.
**Effort (solo dev, Unity):** S = an item (under half a night) · M = about a night · L = several nights.
**Render column:** R = render only · R* = render only, reads sim state · SIM = changes gameplay or the loop (GG call,
golden vectors).
**What I used:** DESIGN-RESEARCH.md §2 #4, ZOMBIES-ULTRAKILL-RESEARCH.md §1, PORT-STATUS.md (FX, UI, Player rows),
four screenshots (n11-hold-ring, n11-relay-lit, n10-armory-wall, n11-results-exfil), `Viewmodel.cs`, `CameraFeel.cs`,
`EnemyViews.cs`, `UiFeel.cs`, `Ui.cs`, `Look.cs`, `config.json`; about 45 web searches and 15 fetched pages. Audio is
GG's: this file names visual hooks only.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **The cheapest big jump is polish the web already tuned and Unity hasn't ported.** `config.json` holds the numbers
   for [V]:
   - the viewmodel's life: bob, sway, breath, the gun's slide / wall tilts, trauma's viewmodel share, the revolver's
     cylinder step, the shotgun's pump, afterimages (`Viewmodel.cs` line 8: "Not ported tonight: bob, sway, breath,
     the slide / wall tilts, trauma"; no `bobAmp` / `sway` / `pump` in `Assets/Scripts/Game`)
   - kill confirm (`fx.killConfirm`), the enemy rim (`fx.enemyRim*`), land puffs, slam rings, sparks, streaks, the
     muzzle flash's point light and per-gun shapes, the impact flare
   - the dusk grade (`post.grade` split-toning), chromatic pulses (hurt, dash, rank, speed), god rays, 900 ambient motes
   - the dash edge tint and the aim-lock FOV pinch (`CameraFeel.cs` line 4: not ported)
2. **Beyond the web, best sellers share four things SKYGRIND lacks:**
   - deaths that read at a glance: DOOM's stagger glow and destructible demons, CoD's head pops and crawlers,
     ULTRAKILL's blood that heals and stays
   - a lit world with depth: distance-ramped fog, contact shadows, light pools on what matters
   - ceremonies: CoD's round change, ULTRAKILL's rank tally, Balatro's counting, Vampire Survivors' chest
   - the settings reviewers check: rebinding, a shake slider, a tilt toggle, an enemy-colour option
3. **The empirical signal:** a study of Steam reviews of top-selling action games found that hit stop, sound coherence
   and camera control separate the best-reviewed from the worst [W]. Dead Cells' post-mortem: "Most reviews talk about
   controls before talking about actual gameplay" [W]. SKYGRIND has hitstop and a camera feel; sound is GG's. So every
   visual hook must fire on the same sim event and frame that GG's audio will bind to [U].
4. **The rule that keeps this safe:** Swink defines polish as effects that "enhance interactions without changing the
   underlying simulation" [S]. That matches SKYGRIND's render-only constraint. Everything below is R or R* unless
   marked SIM.

## 1. Where SKYGRIND stands (what I saw)

- **Screens [V]:** large flat planes in violet and peach with a faint grid; strong directional shadows; cyan edge
  strips (the strongest identity element); a magenta-to-red sky gradient; no visible ambient occlusion, motes or light
  pools.
  - **HUD:** TMP default text in the corners. The objective line is one long string at the top.
  - **ARMORY wall:** reads well (holographic cyan guns, prices, a big orange title).
  - **RESULTS:** eight centred lines at near-equal weight under EXFIL, no hierarchy and no motion.
- **Already in [V]:**
  - the 2-frame muzzle flash (`fx.flashFrames 2`), a tracer (`fx.tracerTime 0.06`)
  - an enemy hit flash (`husk.hitFlashTime 0.08` s white), the stagger lean and pale, the wind-up pulse
  - hit markers (`hud.hitMarkerTime 0.15`, headshot x1.5, kill x1.9), damage arcs, low-HP blink, the hurt vignette
  - the parry flash
  - Eiserloh-style rotational trauma (max 2° pitch / 2° yaw / 1.5° roll, trauma², 25 Hz noise, decay 2.5/s)
  - the FOV ramp (+8° from 8 to 24 m/s, cap 18°), a 10° dash kick (in 0.05 s, out 0.25 s), the landing dip, strafe roll
  - linear sky-tinted fog 45-190 m, bloom, ACES, a vignette
  - the viewmodel's recoil springs, the STITCHER heat glow, the LONGWAVE charge glow
  - speed lines, the momentum arc
- **Missing in the FX / UI rows [V]:** kill confirm, rim glow, dissolve, motes, light pools, blob shadows, skyline,
  damage numbers, rebinding, gamepad, the web's fonts.

## 2. Area by area

### (a) Weapon feel: recoil, viewmodel, flash, tracers, shells, sync, ADS

**What the best do**
- **Exaggerate in first person.** Overwatch's first-person animator stretched McCree's arm out on each shot and pulled
  it back up, "numerous exaggerations ... to add more impact" [S]. MW2019's co-head pushed recoil: "More, I want more.
  I want to feel it; I want the gun to snap" [S].
- **Give each weapon class its own weight; never bolt the gun to the camera.** MW2019's animation director: LMGs stiffer
  than rifles; the head leads and the gun trails; the weapon moves on stairs so it doesn't look "bolted to the camera"
  [S].
- **Nijman's list:** bigger bullets, a muzzle flash, gun kick, camera kick, shells that stay ("permanence"), three
  magnitudes of shake [S].
- **What reviewers single out:**
  - Titanfall 2: guns that feel "weighty as opposed to that floaty feeling" [S].
  - ULTRAKILL: the shotgun's "explosive kick ... like Thor slamming Mjolnir" (preview coverage) [S].
  - DOOM 2016: "every weapon feeling beefy and satisfying" [S].
- **Sync:** the impact-feel study names "time delay between visual and audio feedback" as what breaks sound coherence
  [W].

### (b) Hit feedback: markers, numbers, flash, flinch, crits, kill confirmation, gore

**What the best do**
- **Hitstop scales and is capped.** Sakurai: "the more damage an attack inflicts, the longer the hitstop", with "a cap";
  "both you and your opponent freeze for the exact same amount of time". The victim vibrates, "starting out large and
  growing much smaller by the end", while the hurtbox stays put [W]. Guilty Gear Xrd: light attacks freeze 7 frames,
  heavy about 10 [W].
- **The study:** games without hit stop read as "soft and powerless", with less than 70% positive impact attitude [W].
- **Markers grow a grammar:**
  - CoD Advanced Warfare added a red marker for kills and a double marker for headshots [S].
  - BO6 Zombies: a yellow double-sided crit marker; numbers in white (normal), gold (critical) and blue (armour); every
    piece can be hidden (numbers, health bars, medals, kill feed, score feed) [S].
  - Apex: number colour = the shield tier, yellow = headshot, red = flesh, with a crack when the shield breaks [S].
  - Overwatch: red flares on the crosshair and a "ting" for a headshot [S].
- **Damage numbers cut both ways.** They help in PvE / RPG shooters for crits and effective range. They clutter the
  target, worst with automatics. Destiny 2 players complain they can't turn them off [S]. ULTRAKILL and DOOM show none;
  ULTRAKILL's style feed does that job (its HUD options list "Style Info") [W].
- **Enemies show the damage:**
  - DOOM 2016: a staggered demon glows blue, then orange when you're in glory-kill range [S].
  - Glory kills fit in 30-60 frames so they don't break flow (art director Hara) [W].
  - DOOM Eternal's destructible demons shed flesh and armour as they take damage [S].
  - Halo Infinite: shields glow on damage, the outline flashes bright on the break, and sparks follow [S].
  - CoD: explosives blow legs off into crawlers [S].
- **Kills stay:** ULTRAKILL heals at close range (a punch: 10 torso / 20 limb / 30 crit; a parry: full) [W] and keeps up
  to 500k bloodstains [W]. Nijman's "permanence": bodies stay [S].

### (c) Enemy readability: silhouettes, telegraphs, spawns, rim light

**What the best do**
- **Valorant's clarity shader [W]:**
  - "enemies have a red fresnel, and allies have a neutral, blue fresnel", weighted to up-facing surfaces
  - distant characters brightened, their fresnel "larger and more distinct", clamped
  - one directional light
  - the 144 fps bar: "6.9 milliseconds or less"
  - also: environment materials kept "similar in value", and lights "spotlight" the spaces that matter [S]
  - an enemy highlight colour setting: red (default), purple, yellow, yellow [S]
- **Silhouettes and shape language:** Overwatch heroes must read by outline at distance [S].
- **One colour, one meaning:** in DOOM: The Dark Ages only green projectiles can be parried, because "the human eye is
  very sensitive to the color green" [S]. ULTRAKILL flashes attacks that are hard to read or that open a parry [S]. It
  also offers an "Enemy Silhouettes" assist (none / outline / full, activation distance, thickness) [W].
- **Simplify near the player:** in Returnal / Saros, bullets near the player drop fire and electricity effects and keep
  "a very simple shape and colour" [S]. Homing bullets leave "a long trail that would linger"; tentacles "telegraph
  enemy states" [W].
- **Affixes read by colour plus a shape:** Risk of Rain 2's elites: Blazing red with horns, Glacial white with a crown,
  Overloading blue with a spike row [S].
- **Use the space itself:** Neon White: "Doors are always red and always on the main path, green ivy means you're going
  in the right direction" [W].

### (d) Movement feel: FOV, roll, bob, speed, landings, dash, wall ride, slide, blur

**What the best do**
- **Titanfall [W]:**
  - tilt the view away from the wall during a wall run
  - tilt "as a player starts to jump towards a wall but before they begin to run, allowing them to anticipate it"
  - a view spring on landing whose depth "is relative to the height fallen", which made "landing feel way more real,
    even though ... the movement code, was identical"
  - Titanfall 2 also rotates the view gently away from the wall [S]
- **Mirror's Edge:**
  - keep the camera still during basic locomotion and animate it in climbs, vaults and fights
  - a reticle dot steadied by a distant focal point
  - a wider FOV and less head bob [S]
  - the clean white city was partly a fix for motion sickness [S]
- **Comfort options:**
  - Apex added a "sprint view shake" option for motion sickness [S]
  - ULTRAKILL ships "Camera Tilt", "Screenshake" 0-200%, FOV 45-160 and "Reduce HUD Motion" [W]
  - motion blur is "one of the first things players turn off" [S]

### (e) World and lighting: a premium stylized low-poly look

**What the best do**
- **Lighting beats texture.** Valheim pairs low-res textures with modern lighting, fog and post. Its developers say
  composition and lighting matter more than textures or polygons [S].
- **Contrast, and real colours.** Deep Rock Galactic's facets started as a cave-generation limit, then fit the game. It
  is "fully dependent on stark contrasts between light and dark" and "never uses shaders to tint the entire screen"
  [S].
- **Fog as colour, not grey.** Firewatch samples a 1D colour ramp by distance, with alpha as density, for "nice
  silhouettes off in the distance" (Jane Ng, GDC 2015) [S].
- **Light only what matters.** Valorant: one directional light; environment values kept close; lights on the spaces that
  matter; character readability first [W][S].
- **Negative space.** Neon White: "a clean, low detail look with lots of negative space worked really well" [W].
- **Retro as an option.** ULTRAKILL's PSX look is a set of options: downscaling (720p to 36p), dithering, texture and
  vertex warping, colour compression, a custom palette [W]. Risk of Rain 2 uses cel-style outlines, and colour marks
  danger [S].
- **Particles that react.** In Returnal, impacts make "nearby particles like leaves or sparks ... fly away from the
  impact point" [W].

### (f) UI / UX: HUD motion, menus, title, results, round change, banners, feeds

**What the best do**
- **The round change is a ritual.** CoD Zombies counts rounds in tally marks (1-10 in WaW / BO1 / BO2, 1-5 in BO3),
  then numerals. At a round's end a transition sting plays, and the marks "flash white until the next round starts";
  BO3 "simply burns in a new tally/number" [S].
- **The rank tally:** ULTRAKILL's level end grades time, kills and style D to S, then gives an aggregate, and P for all
  S with no restarts [S].
- **Counting with escalation:** Balatro's "numbers tick up, with escalating sound effects", each card steps forward in
  turn, and the score catches fire on big hands: "screen shake + particle burst + number animation + sound effect = one
  moment" [S].
- **The chest:** in Vampire Survivors everything pauses, lights swirl, rewards cycle "like a slot machine" and a gold
  counter ticks. The developer ("a super rewarding and over-the-top treasure chest animation") came from slot machines
  [S].
- **Themed menus, identity before text.** Hades: every menu is animated to its theme, and a boon's god is "audible
  before it is readable" [S].
- **Minimal HUDs.** Neon White's HUD gives "only the required information"; its cards read by a gun silhouette [W].
  BO6 lets players hide every HUD feed [S].
- **The title screen is the first impression.** Players judge within seconds; a static title "looks really bland";
  some menus show the player's last scene [S].

### (g) The first five minutes: onboarding, controls, settings, performance, Steam Deck

**What the best do**
- **Controls first.** Dead Cells: "Permadeath: never blame the game for poor controls!"; "Take care of details,
  especially if they are not part of the challenge" [W].
- **Forgiveness.** Celeste's coyote time, jump buffering, half gravity at the jump's peak and corner correction all
  "fudge" in the player's favour [S].
- **Response time.** Swink: real-time control needs the game's half of the response under 100 ms [S]. In Claypool's
  studies, performance falls as latency rises, and frame rate matters most for shooting [S].
- **Settings reviewers check:**
  - an FOV slider, uncapped fps, rebinding, ultrawide, a motion-blur toggle [S]
  - the Game Accessibility Guidelines' basics: remappable controls, sensitivity, game speed, "no essential information
    is conveyed by a fixed colour alone", "avoid flickering images", a readable default font size [W]
- **Steam Deck Verified:** the smallest text at least 9 px tall at 1280 x 800 and body text 12 px; glyphs match the
  active input [S].

## 4. Pitfalls

- **Clutter:** numbers over the target hide headshots (Destiny) [S]. Keep the numbers optional; at most one marker per
  shot group.
- **Control loss:** Helldivers 2's explosions are praised as "downright spectacular" [S], but its ragdolls draw "80
  meters in the air" complaints [S]. Spectacle must never move the camera or the player beyond what the sim does.
- **Motion sickness:** Mirror's Edge and Apex both had to calm the camera [S]. Every camera effect gets a slider or a
  toggle, and a single "reduce motion" preset.
- **Colour reservation:**
  - one meaning per colour: cyan = player-usable (parry glint, stagger read, guides); red-orange = enemies and danger
    (kill markers, globs, spawns); magenta = RIVAL only
  - gold is free for crits and points
  - never encode by colour alone: add shape (double ticks, rings) [W: GAG]
- **Determinism:** cosmetic randomness (gib spin, shell scatter, flash roll) must never draw from the run's `Mulberry32`
  stream, or it changes gameplay. Use a separate render stream, seeded per run, so replays also look the same [U].
- **Frame cost:** bench each item; at the u3-n11 bench the worst 1% was 5.55 ms against a 6.9 ms 144 fps bar [V][W].
