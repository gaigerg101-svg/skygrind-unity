# Arena identity: personality, purposeful dressing, a home for the boss (wave 45 research, for GG's J4)

2026-10-17. GG: "if it arena related please dont just randomly plce stuff it needs intension and personality". Not
repeated: loops, introduce->escalate, 4-2, Neon White ambiguity (wave 43 encounters); Valve saturation, route/threat/world
(wave 43 ui-art); Doom canyons, LDB counts (wave 42); Kishotenketsu, Ravenholm saw blade (wave 36); Leviathan, Earthmover,
Mara (wave 37). [fetched] = page read (small-model summary, quotes as returned); [snippet] = search text only.

## A. What gives an arena a personality (Q1)

1. **The arena and its fight are designed as one document.** Shadow of the Colossus: "We started out by creating the
   Colossi's arenas, then figuring out where in the expansive field they would be placed" (Hasegawa); "what kind of arena
   would be necessary based on what form a Colossus would take" (Niwa); Suzuki's one doc held the colossus' shape, the
   battle flow AND the arena. [fetched] https://www.siliconera.com/graphic-designers-discuss-creating-shadow-of-the-colossus-majestic-world/
   - for SKYGRIND: no arena is blocked out before a one-page card names its fight (section D). Geometry is the last field.
2. **A place fantasy that explains the verb.** Ravenholm's early names were "Traptown" / "phystown"; it is "littered with
   traps" built by its lone resident Grigori, and the gravity gun "ultimately ended up defining the level"; Casali framed
   it as a "sanctuary gone bad". [fetched] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenholm
   - for SKYGRIND: answer "who built this place and why" so the ride walls / pads / chimneys are the place's machinery
     (a foundry's crane rails = ride lines, its flue = the chimney climb), not props added for the bot's gates.
3. **The LDB's three worldbuilding questions:** who made the place, who lives there now, how can the player affect it,
   each with "how will the player know"; "story solves game problems"; "worldbuild only what you need".
   [fetched] https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/preproduction/worldbuilding.md
   - for SKYGRIND: three one-line answers per arena on its card; the third must be a gameplay action (break, raise, flood).
4. **A biome rule that every room obeys.** Hades (Ed Gorinstein): Tartarus = "medium-sized chambers that are almost
   always completely walled in" (extra Wall Slam damage, forgiving for the Cast); Asphodel = lava archipelagos, "vast and
   horizontal", each enemy with a "dedicated way of getting over magma"; small rooms early in a biome, large ones late.
   [fetched] https://kotaku.com/hades-level-design-is-less-random-than-it-seems-1845254545
   - for SKYGRIND: each level gets one spatial law (walled / islands / shafts / rings) and one enemy-crossing rule that
     follows from it; a room that breaks the law must be the level's twist, named as such.
5. **A layer = a place = a hazard; a level = a title.** ULTRAKILL's levels carry song-like names ("God Damn the Sun",
   "Waves of the Starless Sea", "Garden of Forking Paths") [fetched] https://ultrakill.wiki.gg/wiki/Level ; Wrath is a
   storm on the Ocean Styx where the water bounces V1 (20 dmg) but instantly kills enemies ("+ Crushed").
   [fetched] https://ultrakill.wiki.gg/wiki/5-2
   - for SKYGRIND: the hazard is the place's own substance and it cuts both ways (molten ash hurts the player, kills a
     knocked-back husk and pays style). Name each arena with a title that names its fantasy, not its function.
6. **The player's action changes the arena.** 5-2: placing the Blue Skull "makes the ferry rise out of the water"; the
   Red Skull raises boarding platforms. 5-4: stepping onto the boat makes the stone section "sink into the water" and the
   Leviathan rises. 7-1: taking the Red Skull silences the music and "The World Looks White" turns to "The World Looks
   Red". [fetched] https://ultrakill.wiki.gg/wiki/5-2 , https://ultrakill.wiki.gg/wiki/5-4 , https://ultrakill.wiki.gg/wiki/7-1
   - for SKYGRIND: every arena has one state change triggered by the player (a valve, a pickup, the last kill of wave 2)
     with a light / colour flip (dusk violet -> furnace orange) so the change reads with the sound off (GG owns sound).
7. **Combat as a puzzle worth solving.** Hugo Martin: "We want combat puzzles that are worth your time to solve and
   master"; Slayer Gates are a "notch or two higher" and "baptisms in fire". [fetched]
   https://www.shacknews.com/article/116635/hell-razer-the-making-of-doom-eternal?page=8
   - for SKYGRIND: the card's "30 s problem" must be a puzzle statement ("the flyers sit over the pit: ride the rim to
     reach them"), not a count ("12 husks").
8. **Identity can come from one small clarity fix.** Neon White's Smackdown took "more than 50" iterations; a metal-bars
   idea "felt so important but it was actually completely unimportant"; "when those two planks got put down, that's when
   it went from kind of a bad level to just about perfect" (Honor); "every card has to have an obvious purpose in the
   moment that you pick it up" (Esposito). [fetched] https://gameinformer.com/feature/2022/11/25/how-a-neon-white-level-is-made
   - for SKYGRIND: playtest each arena's first 10 s blind; cut the piece nobody used, add the one pointer that was missing.
9. **Premise-driven details, and a single hero prop.** Smith / Worch: "The premise generates the events of the story,
   and the events remind players of the premise"; "Every anonymous environmental storytelling moment wastes the
   opportunity to say something about the game"; "A single prop can transform the scene"; "It has to be possible to
   miss some things". [fetched] https://niemanstoryboard.org/2011/01/14/harvey-smith-on-environmental-storytelling-and-embedding-narrative/
   - for SKYGRIND: one hero prop per arena that tells what happened here (a tipped crucible, a snapped crane) AND is
     gameplay (cover, a ride line, a launch). No anonymous crates.

## B. Keeping set dressing purposeful (Q2)

10. **Every rock is an encounter decision.** Griesemer on Silent Cartographer: "I intentionally placed rocks in the way so
    you had to fight them on foot"; remake crates that can be smashed "wreck the encounters"; trees in the WooHoo Jump
    landing: "Lame." [fetched] https://insider-gaming.com/former-halo-dev-criticizes-lame-remake-changes/
    - for SKYGRIND: each solid piece's card row says which encounter or route it forces; a piece that forces nothing is
      cut (GG's "random pillars that serve no purpose", 2026-10-12).
11. **Landmarks must be useful and contrast locally.** LDB Composition: a landmark is "a unique and memorable shape, mass,
    or location"; "landmarks have to feel relevant and useful !! otherwise they don't function as landmarks!"; contrast by
    height, density, orientation (angled off the grid), shape (round among boxes); "The player never plays your screenshot";
    leading lines "routinely fail" in playtests; a vista before a complex arena is "very common".
    [fetched] https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/massing/composition.md
    - for SKYGRIND: one landmark per arena that is ALSO a play object (the boss's ring, the ride tower), visible from the
      entry vista; it breaks the arena's grid by one rule (round, angled or tallest).
12. **Frame the landmark, then move the eye.** HL2: Valve "pointed the door frame outwards into the city square" to frame
    the Citadel, and used birds flying upward; "Players don't look upwards unless something draws their eye"; players look
    where they move and at contrast in colour, shape, light, motion. Ground pieces need a "clear gameplay function"
    (planks off a ledge, train tracks). [fetched] https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/wayfinding.md
    - for SKYGRIND: an upward ride line or chimney is announced by motion going up (embers, sparks) at the entry frame.
13. **Cluster dressing; spend detail on the hero.** "everything in environment art has a readability function"; dress
    "in clusters of related details"; "don't guitar solo a dumpster". [fetched] https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/env-art.md
    - for SKYGRIND: dressing comes in 2-3 clusters per arena, each at a gameplay node (spawn door, refill, ride start).
14. **Prospect / refuge: use it as sequence, but distrust "refuge".** Totten: alternate Prospect (open, exposed; boss
    rooms) and Refuge (enclosed, observe), "ceiling height can give a space the impression of being either"; height with
    safeguards feels safe. [fetched] https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-better-levels-through-human-survival-instincts
    LDB rebuttal: "prospect is real, but refuge is not"; use sightline / vista and cover / enclosure; "No shape or layout is
    intrinsically safe". [fetched] https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/massing/prospect-refuge
    - for SKYGRIND: plan compression -> release (low dark passage -> tall bright arena; architecture's "small, narrow and
      sometimes dark spaces lead to ones that are large and bright" [fetched] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compression_and_expansion);
      safety is a game state (no spawns in view), not a shape.
15. **Molecule first, layout second.** Nodes = spaces, pickups, spawns; edges = relations, thick = more direct, directed =
    one-way (gates, pads, drops); Steiner points = shortcuts, less obvious than the main route; "the more powerful a token
    is, the longer the edge should become"; one example: "between three to five different options" per space; "starting at
    the planar stage more often than not leads to boring, linear progressions". [fetched]
    https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-metrics-of-space-molecule-design
    - for SKYGRIND: draw each arena as a graph (nodes, one-way edges, the one shortcut that skilled movement earns) on its
      card before any Unity piece; a node with no edge purpose is the "random pillar".
16. **Typology sets pace:** corridors give "high control"; slopes down and bright light speed players up, alcoves slow
    them. [fetched] https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/layout/typology.md - for SKYGRIND: type matches verb.

## C. Giving the boss a home (Q3)

17. **The arena is the boss's mechanic.** FFXIV's Hobbes: its "mechanics involve the chamber's walls", so artists designed
    "both a boss and its arena"; "feels like you're fighting the entire arena". [fetched] https://na.finalfantasyxiv.com/blog/003566.html
    - for SKYGRIND: the worm's ring is its body's track: the floor seams it travels in, the plates it cannot break.
18. **Terrain use ramps up.** Ueda: early colossi beatable "without using the terrain", later "you make use of the
    surrounding environment". [fetched] https://glitterberri.com/extended-developer-interview/making-of-the-colossi/
    - for SKYGRIND: boss 1 ignores the terrain, later bosses require it (a pillar to bait into, a ring edge to ride).
19. **Phase by phase, the floor goes away.** Bed of Chaos: after both roots break "the floor starts crumbling" into
    bottomless pits, then a floor chunk falls to expose the jump to the branch. [fetched] https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Bed+of+Chaos
    Radiance: the floor vanishes, "platforms appear in the sky", then a climb. [fetched] https://www.thegamer.com/hollow-knight-radiance-boss-guide/
    - for SKYGRIND: the worm ring loses floor sectors at each phase gate, pushing the fight onto walls / plates.
20. **Open ring edges: punish cheaply.** Moldorm's edgeless floor: a fall drops Link a floor and Moldorm "will regain his
    health and the fight will restart", the fight's top complaint. [snippet] https://zeldauniverse.net/2018/04/05/realm-of-memories-the-frustrating-worm-of-the-tower-of-hera/
    - for SKYGRIND: a fall off the ring costs a bounce + damage (Styx rule, item 5), never boss health.
21. **The home changes after the fight.** Anor Londo's sunlight was an "illusion"; broken, the city shows its "true appearance, shrouded in darkness". [fetched] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anor_Londo
    - for SKYGRIND: a killed boss leaves its arena changed (lit, cooled, opened) and the next room shows it from above.
22. **Leviathan (beyond wave 37):** phase 2's floor is "a large ring formed by the Leviathan's body, with the head in the
    middle". [fetched] https://ultrakill.wiki.gg/wiki/5-4 - for SKYGRIND: the worm's coils can be the last phase's ride rim.
