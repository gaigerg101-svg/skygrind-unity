# GG's request for wave 40 (2026-10-13 ~22:00, after playing the NIGHT39 build n39-r4)

Verbatim (pasted into the forge session):

> When you spawn into a new level, the game needs a short delay or a required movement trigger before the first wave of enemies appears, because right now they spawn far too quickly and you barely have time to react, especially in the third level with the shotgun boost mechanic, which also needs clearer text and better explanation so players understand how to perform the boost properly; I love the mechanic itself, but the description should be more thorough. The rotation animation when firing the pistol needs to be faster and aligned with the actual firing cadence so the animation finishes before the next shot, because currently the aim rotation lags behind and makes the character look visually off. The shotgun also needs a charge shot, and charge shots for both weapons should take longer to load, using a single curved bar under the reticle instead of three small bars, and that same curved bar should fill dynamically based on how much the attack is charged, with this system applied consistently across all weapons. The shotgun's firing animation should also be sped up. The STITCHER weapon should be removed from the fourth level entirely, because the pacing works better if the first three weapons stand alone and the grapple is introduced as the main new mechanic in level four, with the level focusing on mastering that tool rather than juggling multiple new systems at once. For the fourth boss, some areas of the arena should intentionally disable wall-riding—not the whole arena, just select zones—and when the boss enters its second phase, even more areas should restrict wall-riding so players must think smarter about positioning; the boss AI needs to be significantly improved because it often runs out of the map, feels unintelligent, and the model lacks polish, so it needs more animations, clearer telegraphed attacks, and stronger visual cues that encourage players to use the grapple frequently, dodge, reposition, and avoid simply spamming weapons. The game should adopt design principles from movement-centric boss encounters to incentivize mobility. Infinite wall-riding should also be implemented so players don't slowly slide down after a few seconds; the wall-ride must feel crisp, smooth, and reliable. Nearly all rooms in the second level need a complete overhaul to better support the new gauntlet mechanic, because the current layouts feel randomly placed rather than intentionally designed, and the level needs more tutorial-style buildup that teaches the gauntlet's strengths, limitations, and optimal use cases; players should not have access to the pistol at the start of level two, nor the shotgun at the start of level three, and instead unlock them later in the level when entering certain zones, giving players time to truly learn the new weapon. The rooms should be rebuilt to emphasize how the gauntlet excels against large enemies, how its charge attack can be saved for groups of smaller enemies, and how players must manage its cooldown strategically. The game also needs a few more enemy types, including ones that fire projectiles you can deflect, with clear telegraphs and slower approach speeds so they're fun to counter; two additional common enemy types should exist, each encouraging different weapons and playstyles to increase variety and make weapon-switching meaningful. The second boss fight should be completely redesigned into a brawler-style encounter where getting close deals more damage and staying far away reduces pistol effectiveness; it needs entirely new mechanics, new AI, new telegraphs, and a design that encourages gauntlet use while remaining balanced—not too tanky, not too weak. Wall-riding currently triggers too easily when climbing random platforms or stairs, snapping the player to walls too aggressively, so that system needs tuning to feel more controlled and less buggy. Many arenas contain awkwardly placed white rods that you clip through, which looks strange and needs cleanup. Enemy models should be more detailed with clearer silhouettes—whether robotic or more complex shapes—so they feel more defined. The fifth boss fight is extremely buggy and confusing; the arena looks good, but players have no idea what to do, and even I don't know how to damage it, so the fight needs clearer instructions, better attack design, and possibly changes to the arena layout to make progression understandable. The sixth level should be deleted entirely, along with any mention of levels beyond it; the game will have six total levels, but the current sixth one should be removed and rebuilt from scratch later because it looks awful. All of this should be executed using strong design principles, real data, and the best possible methods for organizing a massive build-phase prompt, with the expectation that this project involves extensive construction—five hours of overnight building and one hour of testing, with a maximum of six hours to fully build and have everything functioning.
